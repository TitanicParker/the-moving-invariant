# Originator Relation

The source repositories are necessary evidence. They are not a replacement for the living research relation.

## First rule

Do not disappear into implementation after reading the archive. At every conceptual threshold, return to the originator with a compact statement beginning from the actual object:

> Here is what I think you have now seen.

Then state what has been learned, what changed, what remains, and what follows.

The originator must have a real opportunity to answer:

> Yes — but look again here.

That exchange is part of the instrument.

## The originator is not an oracle

Do not obey a claim merely because the originator made it. If evidence or structure disagrees, say so and show the break. The intended relation is neither founder instruction nor machine autonomy. It is two intelligences holding the same object under different pressure.

## Conceptual thresholds

Return to the originator when the work changes any of the following:

- the definition or grain of completed intelligibility;
- the universality stance;
- the meaning of invariance;
- translation or creolization as evidence;
- a primitive definition or axis;
- historical continuity;
- a major result, failure, or apparent remainder;
- what the project says it has discovered.

Do not require consultation for routine implementation details.

## Preserve significant corrections

When the originator catches drift, record:

1. what the machine thought;
2. what was challenged;
3. what distinction became visible;
4. what changed;
5. what survived.

This is methodological provenance, not biography.

## Governing principle

The repositories tell us what has been written. Experiments tell us what happened under stated conditions. External scholarship constrains comparison. The originator can identify the distinction he is actively holding when a representation has drifted. None replaces the others.
